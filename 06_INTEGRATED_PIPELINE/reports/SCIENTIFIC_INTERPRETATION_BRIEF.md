# RADBIO_NEURO Scientific Interpretation Brief

This brief provides a polished interpretation of the results generated by the integrated physics-to-biology-to-neural pipeline and clarifies the modeling boundaries.

---

## 1. What the Model Currently Proves
- **Comparative Shielding Efficacy**: Shows that increasing equivalent aluminium shielding thickness from 1 mm to 20 mm decreases the absorbed tissue dose and dose-rate by orders of magnitude for both LEO and belt-crossing orbits.
- **Pipeline Functionality**: Validates the computational workflow linking physical particle radiation models to cell damage rates, cellular energy production (ATP), and functional integrate-and-fire neural network simulations.
- **Scaffold Robustness**: Establishes a mathematically stable kinetic framework where extreme exposures lead to functional suppression rather than numerical overflow.

## 2. What the Model Does Not Prove
- **No Direct Clinical Prediction**: Does **not** predict absolute human cognitive impairment, neurological diseases, or clinical astronaut diagnoses.
- **No Experimental Validation**: The parameters represent a mathematical scaffold rather than direct cellular measurements. The exact trajectories are illustrative, not experimentally proven.
- **No High-LET Quality Factor Impact**: Because absorbed dose is used as input, the model does not account for the biological effectiveness (RBE) of high-energy cosmic ions.

## 3. Interpretation of LEO Results
- **Status**: **Calibration Domain Baseline**.
- **Interpretation**: In circular LEO baseline scenarios (such as ISS-like habitats), shielding depths above 5 mm Al reduce dose rates to the $[0.1, 10.0]$ mGy/day range. This is within our primary biological validation domain. Under these conditions, the cellular ROS burden remains low, mitochondrial integrity is preserved, and the modeled neural population maintains stable firing rates.

## 4. Interpretation of Van Allen Results
- **Status**: **Extrapolation / Stress-Test Only**.
- **Interpretation**: The perigee/apogee crossing scenario involves extremely high exposure rates. Behind thin shielding (1 mm or 2 mm Al), dose rates exceed $100$ mGy/day. Under these conditions, the modeled ROS burden rises sharply, leading to total mitochondrial/ATP collapse and complete suppression of integrate-and-fire neural network activity. A blank or zero-firing VAB raster plot reflects this severe stress state. Because these rates are orders of magnitude above our chronic validation data, these projections represent a severe mathematical extrapolation.

## 5. Why Monte Carlo Effective Dose is Not Currently Used
- The raw SPENVIS Monte Carlo (Mulassis) outputs contain `NaN` values for the final integrated effective-dose fields. Using these values would propagate non-numeric errors down the pipeline. They have been audited and audited only; they are flagged as unusable.

## 6. Why SHIELDOSE-2 Tissue Dose is Used
- The SHIELDOSE-2 absorbed dose outputs are fully numeric and complete across all shielding depths. This provides a reliable and continuous physics input layer.

## 7. What Needs Real Validation Next
- **RBE Integration**: Incorporating radiation quality factors (LET/RBE) to scale the absorbed dose based on the radiation type.
- **Experimental Fit Anchors**: Digitizing and integrating more cell-line and animal data from literature to anchor ROS production and mitochondrial decay rates.
- **Electrophysiological Calibration**: Testing integrate-and-fire parameters against real patch-clamp recordings from irradiated brain tissue.

## 8. How This Can Become a Product
- **Operational Habitat Tool**: Scaling the code to parse custom spacecraft CAD geometries, returning localized tissue-dose estimates and projected functional neural risks for astronauts.
- **Mission Planning API**: Integrating these REST endpoints directly with spaceflight tracking systems to provide real-time dashboard alerts during solar particle events (SPE) or belt crossings.
